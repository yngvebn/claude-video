# Style: cinematic film essay

*(story-video original · built-in look `cinematic`)*

A narrated film rather than a trailer. The voice carries the story, so the picture can be patient. Wide 2.39:1 frames
of graded, atmospheric scenery move slowly in depth. Titles are set in a light editorial serif and rack into focus when
their words are spoken. Silhouettes stand in for people and objects. Chapters open on black slates, and subtitles carry
the narration. It's Terrence Malick meets a TED talk: unhurried, warm, a little grand.

## Signature
| Item | On screen |
|---|---|
| Frame | 2.39:1 letterbox (black bars top and bottom); vignette; fine film grain cycling at 24 fps |
| Scenery | 2.5D parallax layers (far haze, far ridge or skyline, subject plane, dark foreground) with atmospheric perspective: far layers lighter and closer to the horizon colour |
| Light | A low sun or moon with glow, a thin horizontal **anamorphic streak** (blue-white), a few wide, faint volumetric rays, dust floating in the light |
| Grade | Teal shadows and amber highlights: dusk, dawn, night or day sky gradients |
| Focus | Shallow depth of field: layers away from the focus plane go soft, and **rack focus** pulls land on anchors |
| Type | Light serif (Cormorant Garamond) for titles, with slight halation glow and letter-spacing that closes as a title fades up; small tracked sans for context lines |
| Subjects | Pure black silhouettes (figures, a lamp with its cone of light, a bottle, a monolith, trees) rim-lit from the sun side |
| Chapters | A black slate with a roman numeral and an italic title, then the scene fades up from black; every chapter ends by fading to black |
| Narration | Cinema subtitles: the current phrase, whole, low in the frame |

## Direction
Build each chapter as one continuous shot: a slow dolly forward with a gentle lateral pan through layered scenery, never
a cut. Choose the scenery for the chapter's feeling: a city at dusk for something that went wrong, an open plain for
people and gatherings, the sea at dawn for resolution. Keep one idea per title, set large in a light serif, and let
titles breathe with long holds. Use silhouettes to make the story physical: a row of figures where some fade out, one
figure facing the sea. Pull focus to the far layer for a reveal and to the foreground for introspection. Motion is
slow and eased; nothing snaps. The score is a sparse piano or strings over a warm pad, and sound design is limited to
room tone and the occasional swell.

## Narration
The voice sets the length of every shot. Titles appear on their anchor words, rack focus lands on anchors, and figures
fade out on the words that say they're gone. Subtitles show the narration phrase by phrase, so on-screen titles should
add emphasis rather than repeat it.
